How tooth decay develops and how to prevent it

Understanding tooth decay, fluoride and eating habits.

Updated: 21.11.2026 · Oral health information

How decay starts

Oral bacteria turn sugars in food and drink into acids. Repeated acid exposure can remove minerals from enamel and eventually lead to a cavity.

Symptoms may appear late

Early tooth decay may cause no pain. Sensitivity to hot, cold or sweet foods, pain when chewing or a visible spot may develop later. A dental examination, sometimes with imaging, is needed to assess the tooth.

Everyday prevention

Brush with fluoride toothpaste, clean between teeth and reduce how often you consume sugary snacks or drinks. Frequent sugar exposure gives acids more opportunities to affect the enamel.

Why early assessment helps

Some early mineral loss may be managed with fluoride and changes in habits. More advanced decay may need a filling or another procedure; the appropriate plan depends on examination.
